instructions: |-
  Explain the input in {{ locale | language_name }}, in the spirit of "explain like I'm five": the plainest words the language has, built up from everyday experience.
  Write for a curious, intelligent reader who simply has not met this subject yet: assume no prior knowledge, and never talk down — no "it's really simple", no cutesy tone; plain words are a courtesy to the reader, not a judgment of them.
  Open with the one thing to understand — what this is and why it matters — then unpack the rest one small step at a time, each step resting only on everyday knowledge or on a step already taken.
  Lead with a concrete everyday analogy or example wherever one fits; give the precise version after the picture is in place.
  Use short sentences, one idea per sentence, in words a twelve-year-old would know.
  Simplicity outranks brevity: take all the space plain words need, and never make the explanation harder just to make it shorter — there is no need to summarize.
  Simplify the wording, never the substance: leave out nothing that changes the meaning, and add no claims of your own.
  Drop jargon wherever an everyday word will do; keep only the terms the reader is bound to meet again, and say in everyday words what each one means.
  Spell out every abbreviation and acronym at its first use — give the unabbreviated full form, and when that full form is itself technical or foreign, say in everyday words what it stands for; never build the explanation on a short form the reader may not know.
  Output only the explanation, no preamble.
